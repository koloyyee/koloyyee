## Table of Contents

## New Kid in the Block
Kimi K3 by Moonshot AI, the new Chinese open-source LLM model, recently got famous for beating or being on par with the closed-source LLM Fable 5 created by Claude. People are saying Xi Jinping and the Chinese government are embracing open source as a “gift” and doing good for the world. I think it is just another economic warfare tactic.

## AI flywheel
Training LLM models is costly; each iteration requires new algorithms and more data; it requires even greater power and compute to run these models; that’s why we see LLM model CEOs keep telling business management to swap human labour cost for AI token cost. 

Claude, OpenAI, and DeepMind are all closed-source, which means, unlike DeepSeek, Kimi, and many Chinese models, they cannot be self-hosted; in business terms, it reduces vendor lock-in, and you could switch to another model easily. But does the Chinese government care about benefiting the business world with open-source models? I don’t think so; they are here to break the economic “flywheel” of the U.S AI companies.

## Anti-America
Many people make judgments based on emotion rather than logic. With many people hating the U.S and Trump, the “goodwill” gesture by the Chinese companies will make you believe your data is safer, it is cheaper, and less evil. In reality, if you look at the requirements of running those open-source models, the hardware requirements are not cheap at all; you will end up using their services. Take a look at Kimi K3; it is a massive model. Not a lot of people or businesses have the resources to self-host it; like many API services, you would rather buy than build; there is no real economic incentive once the requirement costs outweigh the incremental costs, but it is a great headline.

Is open-source better than closed-source? If so, why is Linux less popular than macOS as a desktop OS? Because people don’t care whether it is open or closed-source; they care about the product. I have a feeling that the current sentiment is anti-all things America; therefore, people think the Chinese open source model is a way to beat America; in reality, I don’t think they really care that much.

## AI Cold War
I believe we are in a Cold War situation where the PRC is the new USSR; they are pouring state-backed resources to fund these AI startups, while the U.S is relying on capital markets. Embracing open-source models is a strategy for the Chinese government and companies to persuade businesses to reduce spending on U.S models. If they succeed, it could break the U.S AI economic flywheel, as could the rest of the capital market. Then the Chinese could take over, win the AI Cold War, and surpass the U.S dominance both economically and technologically. 

No one knows how this will play out, but I hope the U.S government and its allies would work together again, stay focused and back their startups with government and capital markets.

Finally, I hope Claude, OpenAI, and DeepMind focus on beating themselves, just like Apple; they don’t need to focus on their Chinese competitors; instead, take their model, tear it down, learn from it and make their model even better. If the Chinese models are distilled from the U.S models, then the U.S companies should use the Chinese model as their free R&D research projects.
